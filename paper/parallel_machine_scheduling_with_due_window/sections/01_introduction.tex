\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Manufacturers facing volatile demand must decide not only how to allocate and
sequence jobs on their internal machines, but also which jobs should be transferred
to an external provider. These decisions are particularly interdependent in
just-in-time production. Completing a job before its due window may create inventory
or holding costs, whereas completing it after the window may cause service failures
or contractual penalties. Sequence-dependent setups further couple the jobs assigned
to a machine. Outsourcing can relieve internal congestion, but it is not necessarily
priced job by job: providers often offer quantity discounts based on the total value
of the outsourced work. Consequently, the marginal outsourcing cost of a job depends
on the other jobs outsourced with it.

Due-window scheduling and production outsourcing have largely developed as separate
research streams. Due-window models capture the operational consequences of early
and late completion and have been studied under a wide range of machine environments
and cost criteria \citep{wan2002tabu,rosa2017algorithms,kramer2019unified}.
Exact methods for parallel-machine scheduling have progressed from problem-specific
branch-and-bound procedures to column-generation and branch-and-price methods in
which a column represents a complete single-machine schedule
\citep{kedad2008lowerbounds,sen2015preemptive,bulhoes2020exact}. Research on
scheduling with outsourcing, by contrast, has mainly focused on deciding which jobs
are processed internally and which are assigned to external resources, often using
additive outsourcing charges or heuristic solution methods
\citep{lee2008outsourcing,zhong2022coordinated,lu2021discount}. These models do not capture
the combination considered here: job-dependent due windows, sequence-dependent
internal setups, and a nonseparable aggregate outsourcing tariff.

The nonseparable tariff changes both the formulation and the solution method. If an
outsourced subset is represented by a single column, its pricing problem becomes a
subset-selection problem with an aggregate concave cost. If outsourcing decisions
are kept explicitly in the master problem, the tariff must instead be represented by
segment variables and the branch-and-price tree must enforce a consistent segment.
This distinction is analogous to the alternative private-fleet/common-carrier
formulations of \citet{dabia2019rich}, but the internal columns in the present problem
are schedules rather than vehicle routes, and their costs require optimizing the
completion times of a fixed job sequence.

The internal pricing problem presents a second difficulty. A time-indexed graph gives
an exact dynamic program when all time data are integral, but its size grows with the
numerical time horizon. A continuous-time formulation avoids this dependence.
However, a label must then carry a function of completion time rather than a scalar
cost. We represent this function explicitly as a piecewise-linear lower envelope and
combine forward and backward labels at an adaptively selected midpoint. To control
elementarity without placing the full visited set in every label, we use an ng-route
relaxation and a decremental state-space refinement procedure, following the general
pricing principles of \citet{martinelli2014pricing}. The refinement terminates only
when an elementary negative-reduced-cost schedule has been found or the relaxed
problem itself certifies that no such schedule exists.

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item We formulate an integrated parallel-machine scheduling and outsourcing
    problem with job-dependent due-window costs, sequence-dependent internal setups,
    and an aggregate quantity-discount tariff.

    \item We develop two set-covering column-generation formulations that place the
    outsourcing structure on different sides of the decomposition: an
    outsourcing-column formulation and a formulation with explicit outsourcing and
    tariff variables in the master problem.

    \item We develop a continuous-time exact pricing algorithm based on
    bidirectional piecewise-linear labeling and ng-route decremental state-space
    refinement. The method does not require discretizing the time horizon.

    \item We integrate the pricing routines into a branch-and-price framework with
    heuristic pricing, certified node bounds, completion-bound and time-indexed
    reduced-cost fixing, formulation-specific branching, strong-branching trials,
    and Phase-I feasibility repair. An exact time-indexed pricing algorithm is
    retained as an alternative for integer-time instances.
\end{enumerate}
