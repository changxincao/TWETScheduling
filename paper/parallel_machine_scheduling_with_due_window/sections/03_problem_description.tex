\section{Problem Description}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Internal production}

Let $\mathcal J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ be a set of jobs and let
$\mathcal M=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ be a set of identical parallel machines. All jobs and
machines are available at time zero. Each job must either be processed internally on
one machine or outsourced. Internal processing is nonpreemptive, and a machine
processes at most one job at a time.

Job $j\in\mathcal J$ has processing time $p_j$ and due window
$[d_j^e,d_j^l]$. If job $j$ follows job $i$ on the same machine, the transition
requires setup time $s_{ij}$ and incurs setup cost $\kappa_{ij}$. Index $0$ denotes
the initial state of a machine; hence $s_{0j}$ and $\kappa_{0j}$ are the initial
setup time and cost of job $j$. Idle time is allowed between consecutive jobs.

The completion-time penalty of job $j$ is a nonnegative, finitely represented
piecewise-linear function $\phi_j(t)$. The principal case is weighted
earliness--tardiness with a due window,
\begin{equation}
    \phi_j(t)
    =
    \alpha_j[d_j^e-t]^+
    +
    \beta_j[t-d_j^l]^+,
    \qquad [x]^+:=\max\{x,0\},
    \label{eq:due-window-penalty}
\end{equation}
but the pricing algorithms only require that sums, translations, lower envelopes,
and interval minima of the represented functions can be evaluated exactly. This
also covers due dates as zero-width windows and several bounded or piecewise-linear
early/late-work penalties.

An internal machine schedule is an elementary sequence
$r=(j_1,\ldots,j_q)$ of distinct jobs. Let $j_0=0$ and $C_{j_0}=0$. Its cost is
\begin{equation}
    c_r^{\mathrm{in}}
    =
    \sum_{h=1}^{q}\kappa_{j_{h-1}j_h}
    +
    \min_{\boldsymbol C}
    \left\{
        \sum_{h=1}^{q}\phi_{j_h}(C_{j_h})
        :
        C_{j_h}\ge
        C_{j_{h-1}}+s_{j_{h-1}j_h}+p_{j_h}
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:internal-column-cost}
\end{equation}
The inequalities in \eqref{eq:internal-column-cost} permit intentional idle time.
Thus, a column specifies a job order, while its cost already contains the optimal
timing of that order.

\subsection{Aggregate outsourcing tariff}

If job $j$ is outsourced, it leaves the internal schedule and has baseline
outsourcing amount $b_j\ge0$. For an outsourced set $O\subseteq\mathcal J$, define
\begin{equation}
    B(O)=\sum_{j\in O}b_j.
\end{equation}
The actual payment is $G(B(O))$, where $G$ is a nondecreasing concave
piecewise-linear tariff satisfying $G(0)=0$. Let
$\mathcal L=\{1,\ldots,L\}$ be its tariff segments. Segment $\ell$ applies on
$[\underline q_\ell,\overline q_\ell]$ and has affine expression
\begin{equation}
    G(q)=a_\ell q+c_\ell
    \quad
    \text{for }
    q\in[\underline q_\ell,\overline q_\ell].
\end{equation}
Concavity implies nonincreasing marginal rates $a_\ell$. The no-discount case is
the single-segment tariff $G(q)=q$.

The outsourcing tariff is aggregate rather than job-separable. Hence the cost of
outsourcing job $j$ depends on the other outsourced jobs. This coupling motivates
the two formulations in Section~\ref{sec:formulations}.

\subsection{Optimization problem}

A feasible solution consists of at most $m$ elementary internal sequences and one
outsourced set such that every job is covered. Its objective is
\begin{equation}
    \min
    \left\{
        \sum_{r\in R^\star}c_r^{\mathrm{in}}
        +
        G(B(O))
    \right\},
    \label{eq:original-problem}
\end{equation}
where $R^\star$ is the selected set of internal machine schedules and $O$ is the
outsourced job set. The formulations below use covering rows, as in the implemented
restricted master problems.
