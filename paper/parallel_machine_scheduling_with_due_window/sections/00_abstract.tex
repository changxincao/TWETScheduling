\begin{abstract}
We study a parallel-machine scheduling problem in which each job must either be
processed internally within a preferred due window or outsourced. Internal
production incurs sequence-dependent setup times and costs together with
earliness--tardiness penalties, whereas outsourced jobs are charged through a
quantity-discount function of their aggregate baseline outsourcing cost. The
resulting problem jointly determines job sourcing, machine assignment, sequencing,
and completion times. We develop two set-covering formulations. Formulation
\textup{(SP1)} represents both internal schedules and outsourced job sets by
columns; formulation \textup{(SP2)} generates only internal schedules and keeps
the outsourcing and tariff decisions in the master problem. Their common internal
pricing problem is solved by continuous-time bidirectional labeling, with the
reduced cost of a partial sequence represented by a piecewise-linear function.
An ng-route relaxation and decremental state-space refinement recover elementary
schedules without discretizing the time horizon. We embed both formulations in an
exact branch-price-and-cut framework with formulation-specific pricing, branching,
bounds, and Phase-I feasibility repair. For integer-time instances, an exact
time-indexed pricing method provides an alternative whose state space expands with
the numerical time horizon.
\end{abstract}
