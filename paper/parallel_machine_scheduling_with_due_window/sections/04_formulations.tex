\section{Set-Covering Formulations}\label{sec:formulations}

Let $\mathcal R$ be the set of all feasible elementary internal schedules. For
$r\in\mathcal R$, let $a_{jr}$ equal 1 if schedule $r$ contains job $j$ and 0
otherwise, and let $x_r$ indicate whether $r$ is selected. Both formulations use
the machine-capacity constraint
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}x_r\le m.
    \label{eq:machine-capacity}
\end{equation}
They differ in whether the complete outsourced set is represented by a column or
by explicit master variables. This pair of decomposition choices parallels the
private-fleet/common-carrier formulations of \citet{dabia2019rich}, but the
internal columns here are timed machine schedules rather than vehicle routes.

\subsection{Outsourcing-column formulation}
\label{subsec:sp1}

Formulation \textup{(SP1)} represents both internal schedules and outsourced job
sets by columns. Let
$\mathcal O=2^{\mathcal J}\setminus\{\varnothing\}$ be the family of nonempty
outsourced sets. For $o\in\mathcal O$, let $d_{jo}$ equal 1 if job $j$ belongs to
set $o$ and 0 otherwise, and define
\begin{equation}
    c_o^{\mathrm{out}}
    =
    G\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}b_jd_{jo}\right).
    \label{eq:outsourcing-column-cost}
\end{equation}
Variable $w_o$ selects outsourcing column $o$. The formulation is
\begin{align}
\textup{(SP1)}\qquad
\min\quad
    &\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}c_r^{\mathrm{in}}x_r
     +\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}c_o^{\mathrm{out}}w_o
     \label{eq:sp1-obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
    &\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}a_{jr}x_r
     +\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}d_{jo}w_o\ge1
     &&j\in\mathcal J, \label{eq:sp1-cover}\\
    &\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}x_r\le m,
     \label{eq:sp1-machines}\\
    &\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}w_o\le1,
     \label{eq:sp1-at-most-one-outsourcing}\\
    &x_r\in\{0,1\}
     &&r\in\mathcal R,\\
    &w_o\in\{0,1\}
     &&o\in\mathcal O.
\end{align}
Selecting no $w_o$ represents a solution without outsourcing.
Constraint \eqref{eq:sp1-at-most-one-outsourcing} is essential because the
provider prices one aggregate outsourced portfolio rather than several
independently charged subsets.

Let $\pi_j\ge0$ be the dual value of covering row
\eqref{eq:sp1-cover}, $\sigma\le0$ the dual value of the machine-capacity row, and
$\mu\le0$ the dual value of
\eqref{eq:sp1-at-most-one-outsourcing}. The reduced costs of an internal schedule
$r$ and an outsourcing column $o$ are
\begin{align}
    \bar c_r^{\mathrm{in}}
    &=
    c_r^{\mathrm{in}}
    -\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\pi_j a_{jr}
    -\sigma,
    \label{eq:internal-reduced-cost}\\
    \bar c_o^{\mathrm{out}}
    &=
    G\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}b_jd_{jo}\right)
    -\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\pi_jd_{jo}
    -\mu.
    \label{eq:outsourcing-reduced-cost}
\end{align}
After removing the constant $-\mu$, outsourcing pricing is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
    \max_{O\subseteq\mathcal J}
    \left\{
        \sum_{j\in O}\pi_j
        -
        G\left(\sum_{j\in O}b_j\right)
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:outsourcing-subset-pricing}
\end{equation}
Branching rows add the corresponding dual terms to these expressions.

The outsourcing-column representation is not tied to a concave piecewise-linear
tariff. For the aggregate form in \eqref{eq:outsourcing-column-cost}, the
two-dimensional pricing algorithm described in
Section~\ref{subsec:outsourcing-pricing} remains valid for any evaluable
nondecreasing function $G$; neither piecewise linearity nor concavity is required
by its state transitions or dominance rule. Nondecreasingness also ensures that,
with the covering rows used here, an outsourced job already covered internally can
be removed from the outsourcing column without increasing its cost. Concavity
provides the quantity-discount interpretation adopted in the baseline problem,
and the computational implementation instantiates $G$ by the piecewise-linear
tariff in \eqref{eq:outsourcing-tariff-segment}. More generally, an
inclusion-monotone set cost $C^{\mathrm{out}}(O)$ can be assigned directly to
outsourcing columns, provided that an exact pricing oracle compatible with that
cost replaces \eqref{eq:outsourcing-subset-pricing}. A nonmonotone arbitrary set
cost would require a different master representation or partitioning rows and is
outside the present extension.

\subsection{Explicit-outsourcing formulation}
\label{subsec:sp2}

Formulation \textup{(SP2)} generates only internal schedule columns. Let $y_j$
equal 1 if job $j$ is outsourced. Its nonlinear form is
\begin{align}
\textup{(SP2)}\qquad
\min\quad
    & \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}c_r^{\mathrm{in}}x_r
      +G\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}b_jy_j\right)
      \label{eq:sp2-obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
    & \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}a_{jr}x_r+y_j\ge1
      &&j\in\mathcal J, \label{eq:sp2-cover}\\
    & \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}x_r\le m, \label{eq:sp2-machines}\\
    & x_r\in\{0,1\}
      &&r\in\mathcal R,\\
    & y_j\in\{0,1\}
      &&j\in\mathcal J.
\end{align}

The implemented master represents the baseline tariff by segment-selection
variables. Let $z_\ell$ select segment $\ell$ and let $q_\ell$ be the aggregate
baseline cost assigned to it. The tariff term in \eqref{eq:sp2-obj} is replaced by
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}
    \left(\gamma_\ell q_\ell+\delta_\ell z_\ell\right),
    \label{eq:tariff-objective}
\end{equation}
and the following constraints are added:
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}b_jy_j
      =\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}q_\ell,
      \label{eq:tariff-balance}\\
    &\underline q_\ell z_\ell
      \le q_\ell
      \le\overline q_\ell z_\ell
      &&\ell\in\mathcal L,
      \label{eq:tariff-bounds}\\
    &\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}z_\ell=1,
      \label{eq:tariff-one-segment}\\
    &z_\ell\in\{0,1\},\quad q_\ell\ge0
      &&\ell\in\mathcal L.
      \label{eq:tariff-domains}
\end{align}
Only internal schedules are generated dynamically. The outsourcing decisions and
the finite tariff representation remain in the restricted master problem. With
$\pi_j$ denoting the dual value of \eqref{eq:sp2-cover}, internal reduced cost is
again given by \eqref{eq:internal-reduced-cost}.

\subsection{Comparison of the formulations}
\label{subsec:formulation-comparison}

Formulation \textup{(SP1)} separates the master from the detailed outsourcing cost
structure and can accommodate a broad outsourcing-column cost interface. Its cost
is a second combinatorial pricing family and outsourcing-membership branching.
Formulation \textup{(SP2)} avoids outsourcing pricing and has only one generated
column family, but the outsourcing tariff must have a finite master
representation, such as
\eqref{eq:tariff-objective}--\eqref{eq:tariff-domains}. The two formulations
therefore trade a more general but columnized outsourcing side against a richer
explicit master. Their internal columns and internal pricing problem are
identical.
