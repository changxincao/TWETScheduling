\section{Literature Review}\label{sec:literature}

The present study lies at the intersection of four research streams: scheduling
with due-window penalties, exact methods for parallel-machine scheduling,
production scheduling with outsourcing, and relaxed elementary pricing. We review
these streams by the role they play in the proposed model and algorithm.

\subsection{Due-window scheduling and functional timing costs}

A due window specifies an interval of preferred completion times instead of a
single due date. Completing a job before the lower endpoint incurs earliness,
whereas completing it after the upper endpoint incurs tardiness. In the
single-machine setting, \citet{wan2002tabu} combine a timing procedure with tabu
search for distinct due windows and weighted earliness--tardiness penalties.
\citet{rosa2017algorithms} subsequently consider distinct time windows, general
earliness--tardiness penalties, and sequence-dependent setups. Their exact results
require a restricted setup structure, whereas the complete problem is treated
heuristically. The annotated review of \citet{kramer2019unified} places these
models in the broader earliness--tardiness literature and documents the variety of
machine environments, setup assumptions, release dates, and idle-time decisions.

Two methodological implications are important here. First, the cost of a fixed
sequence cannot generally be obtained by simply processing its jobs without idle
time; the completion times must be optimized jointly. Second, the value of a
partial sequence is naturally a function of its boundary time. This functional
view also appears in routing with time-dependent penalties. \citet{ibaraki2008ils}
use dynamic programming to evaluate a fixed route under convex piecewise-linear
time penalties, and \citet{liberatore2011soft} embed soft time-window penalties in
an exact column-generation algorithm. These studies motivate our PWLF
representation, although the present pricing problem concerns machine schedules
rather than vehicle routes.

\subsection{Exact methods for parallel-machine scheduling}

For parallel-machine earliness--tardiness scheduling,
\citet{kedad2008lowerbounds} develop assignment-based and time-indexed lower bounds
for distinct due dates. \citet{sen2015preemptive} derive a strong preemptive
relaxation for weighted tardiness and earliness--tardiness problems on unrelated
parallel machines and use it within a Benders framework. These contributions show
the value of problem-specific relaxations, but they do not provide a general exact
framework for the combination studied here.

Column generation offers a different decomposition: one master column represents a
complete schedule on one machine. \citet{bulhoes2020exact} develop a generic
branch-cut-and-price framework for a broad class of parallel-machine scheduling
problems and integrate strong branching, reduced-cost fixing, dual stabilization,
and rank-1 cuts. Their work is the closest exact-method predecessor of our machine
side. The present problem adds two features that materially change this framework.
The internal pricing cost is a continuous piecewise-linear function induced by
job-dependent due windows and sequence-dependent setups, and the master must also
decide which jobs are outsourced under a nonseparable tariff.

\subsection{Scheduling with acceptance, rejection, and outsourcing}

Order acceptance and scheduling jointly decide which jobs enter the production
system and how the accepted jobs are processed. \citet{slotnick2011review} provide
a taxonomy of this literature. More recent work combines acceptance with richer
timing or production structures. For example, \citet{li2021acceptance} include
earliness--tardiness penalties and overtime, while \citet{chen2023lbbd} use
logic-based Benders decomposition for acceptance and scheduling in distributed
manufacturing. These models establish the importance of integrating selection and
scheduling, but rejection is normally represented by an additive job-level loss.

Scheduling with an explicit outsourcing option is closer to the present setting.
\citet{lee2008outsourcing} study due-date-related measures on a single machine with
outsourcing, and \citet{safarzadeh2019jobshop} allow jobs to be outsourced in a job
shop. \citet{kim2022flowshop} introduce outsourcing lead times in a two-machine
flow shop. At a broader system level, \citet{zhong2022coordinated} coordinate
outsourcing, in-house production, and distribution. These studies differ in
machine environment and solution method, but most attach a cost or lead time to
each outsourced job. They do not model the outsourced portfolio through one
aggregate concave tariff.

Aggregate pricing is studied more directly by \citet{lu2021discount}, who analyze
in-house production and outsourcing under alternative discount schemes on total
outsourcing cost. Related routing models provide exact decomposition precedents.
\citet{gahm2017routing} consider private vehicles, multiple common carriers,
volume discounts, and rental options. \citet{dabia2019rich} develop two exact
formulations for a rich private-fleet/common-carrier routing problem: one
columnizes both private routes and common-carrier choices, whereas the other keeps
the outsourcing decisions in the master. \citet{baller2020partial} extend this
line to partial outsourcing and solve two path-based formulations by
branch-price-and-cut. These routing studies motivate our two alternative
outsourcing formulations. Their internal columns, however, are routes; they do not
address the timing of parallel-machine sequences with due-window costs and
sequence-dependent setups.

\subsection{Relaxed elementary pricing and research gap}

Elementarity is a principal source of pricing complexity. Ng-route relaxations keep
only selected visit memories, while decremental state-space relaxation restores
missing memories when repeated-visit witnesses are found.
\citet{martinelli2014pricing} combine ng-route relaxation, DSSR, and completion
bounds to solve elementary and restricted non-elementary route pricing. We adapt
this logic to scheduling, where a label carries a PWLF rather than a scalar cost
and where forward and backward labels must be joined over continuous completion
times.

The literature therefore provides the necessary ingredients separately.
Due-window studies define the timing costs and show the difficulty created by
general setups; parallel-machine research supplies lower bounds and exact
column-generation frameworks; outsourcing studies integrate make-or-buy
decisions; discount and common-carrier models introduce aggregate tariffs; and
routing research supplies ng-DSSR pricing techniques. We are not aware of an exact
method that combines identical parallel machines, job-dependent due windows,
sequence-dependent setup times and costs, an aggregate concave outsourcing tariff,
and exact branch-and-price. The contribution of this paper is to integrate these
elements and to develop the continuous-time functional pricing machinery required
by their combination.
